Project Overview

This site development project integrates a 191 space parking facility with full-size city bus access, ADA accessible parking, pedestrian circulation, landscaping, site lighting, and stormwater management within a constrained parcel along N. Tryon Street. Rather than functioning solely as a conventional parking lot, the site was designed to support both everyday vehicle circulation and public transit operations within the same development footprint. The project demonstrates my ability to approach site design as an interconnected system, where transportation, accessibility, drainage, and land use requirements must work together rather than independently.

Design Considerations

The layout required careful coordination of full size city bus circulation, ADA-accessible spaces, pedestrian crossings, internal drive aisles, landscaped islands, lighting, and an 11,500 sq ft detention pond, all of which competed for usable site area. Typical 9 ft × 18 ft parking stalls and 24 ft drive aisles establish the parking geometry, while varying curb radii and specialized circulation areas respond to the different movements occurring throughout the site. What made the design particularly challenging was preserving efficient parking and circulation while introducing infrastructure that a traditional parking layout would not normally have to accommodate.

Design Approach

I approached the layout as a multimodal circulation and site-optimization problem, rather than simply maximizing the number of parking spaces. The full-size city bus route introduces a different turning envelope and circulation demand than passenger vehicles, requiring its movement to be coordinated with pedestrian access, accessible parking, roadway connections, and the surrounding parking field. Incorporating those transportation demands alongside stormwater detention, and other site requirements created an opportunity to demonstrate how thoughtful geometric planning can make several competing engineering systems function within one cohesive design.
