Academic Project

Residential Site Development I

Site civil / land development - Class

Overview

This academic land development project involved the planning and design of a 77 lot residential subdivision across an approximately 85 acre site, with roughly 48 acres contained within the development boundary. The R-4 residential layout coordinated roadway circulation, lot configuration, existing topography, drainage corridors, and dedicated open space as an interconnected site system rather than independent design elements. Residential parcels were developed around 10,000 sq. ft. minimum lot areas, 120 ft minimum lot depths, typical 50' × 30' building footprints, and 30 ft front, 5 ft side, and 20 ft rear setbacks, with all lots receiving internal street access rather than direct access to Chipper Jones Boulevard.

What's Included

The subdivision incorporates 77 buildable residential lots, an interconnected local street network, multiple cul de sacs, horizontal roadway curves, curb and gutter infrastructure, and a centrally integrated park and open space area. Roadway geometry was evaluated in plan and profile, including station based existing ground profiles extending approximately 1,800 to 2,000 ft along documented roadway segments, allowing horizontal alignment, vertical conditions, lot frontage, and surrounding terrain to be considered together. The overall layout also incorporated applicable Mecklenburg County land development criteria for local residential streets, cul de sacs, culvert crossings, setbacks, and associated roadway infrastructure.

Complications

The primary engineering challenge was achieving 77 viable residential lots while working around variable topography, an existing drainage corridor, roadway geometric constraints, and dedicated open space, all of which reduced or influenced developable land. Changes in existing elevation affected roadway placement, drainage patterns, lot geometry, and usable building envelopes, while the central park and drainage corridor created additional fixed constraints around which the subdivision had to develop. The resulting layout required iterative coordination between roadway alignment, lot boundaries, building envelopes, grading considerations, drainage conveyance, and open space allocation to maintain connectivity and create a functional residential development within the available site.
