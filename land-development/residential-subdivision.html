Academic Project

Residential Subdivision I

Site civil / land development, class project, Roadway & Grading

Detail Sheet →

Overview

This academic project involved planning a 77-lot residential subdivision across an approximately 85-acre site, including approximately 48 acres within the development boundary. The design coordinated residential lots, internal roadway circulation, existing topography, drainage corridors, and open space as an integrated land-development system. The R-4 subdivision incorporated 10,000 sq. ft. minimum lots, 120-ft minimum lot depths, typical 50' × 30' building footprints, and 30-ft front, 5-ft side, and 20-ft rear setbacks. All residential lots were provided internal access, eliminating direct lot access to Chipper Jones Boulevard.

What's Included

The site plan incorporates 77 buildable lots, an interconnected residential street network, multiple cul-de-sacs, horizontal curves, curb-and-gutter infrastructure, and a central park/open-space area. Lot geometry and building envelopes were coordinated with existing contours, roadway frontage, setbacks, and drainage features. Roadway alignments were evaluated in plan and profile, with station-based ground profiles extending approximately 1,800–2,000 ft along documented roadway segments. The design also incorporated Mecklenburg County land-development standards for local residential streets, cul-de-sacs, culvert crossings, and associated roadway infrastructure.

Complications

The primary challenge was fitting 77 residential lots and supporting infrastructure around variable topography, an existing drainage corridor, and dedicated open space while maintaining roadway connectivity and viable building areas. Elevation changes influenced roadway alignment, drainage behavior, and lot configuration, while the central park and drainage corridor reduced the land available for residential development. Balancing these constraints required coordinated adjustments to roadway geometry, lot boundaries, building envelopes, drainage, and open-space allocation to produce a functional overall site plan.
