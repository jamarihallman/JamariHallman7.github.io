Residential Subdivision & Land Development Site Design

Site civil / land development, class project

Site Plan → Roadway & Site Details → Development Notes →

Overview

This project involved the planning and engineering of a 35-lot residential subdivision, integrating roadway access, site grading, stormwater management, and county land-development standards. Rather than treating each lot independently, roadway geometry, grading, drainage, and lot configuration were coordinated as one site-development system. Lots were designed around a 10,000 sq. ft. minimum lot size, typical 45' × 30' building footprint, required setbacks, and R-4 zoning.

What's Included

An internal residential street network incorporating cul-de-sacs, roadway intersections, curb-and-gutter, residential access, and accessible ramps provides circulation throughout the development while keeping individual lots from directly accessing the adjacent major roadway. Existing topography was incorporated into the layout to coordinate roadway alignment, lot placement, and drainage. Dedicated stormwater management areas were also integrated into the site to accommodate runoff while preserving functional residential development.

Complications

The primary challenge was balancing competing site requirements, including existing topography, stormwater management, setbacks, roadway geometry, and usable lot area within one coordinated layout. Elevation changes influenced roadway alignment and drainage behavior, requiring the street network to respond to both the desired circulation pattern and the site's physical constraints. Accommodating roadway and stormwater infrastructure while maintaining viable residential lots required design tradeoffs across the overall development.
