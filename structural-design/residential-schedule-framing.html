Residential Building Structural Modeling & Construction Schedule

Academic Project | Construction Engineering / BIM & Project Controls

Two-Story Residential Building

A 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building developed on a 30 ft × 40 ft lot in Charlotte, NC, as provided in the assignment brief. My work translated this design into a coordinated Primavera P6 construction schedule and reviewed the accompanying floor and site plans.

The Primavera P6 schedule established a 66-day construction sequence consisting of a 50-day substructure phase, 14-day Level 1 phase, and 2-day Level 2 phase. Activities shown in the schedule carry zero total float, creating a tightly constrained sequence in which delays to predecessor activities could directly affect the planned completion date.

The site plan establishes the building's placement on the 30 ft × 40 ft lot, including setbacks, access, and the fenced site boundary shown in the assignment drawings. Reviewing this plan alongside the schedule made clear how site constraints shaped the construction sequencing decisions.

The second floor plan lays out the upper level's room arrangement and structural bearing points relative to the floor below. Coordinating this layout with the ground floor plan was necessary to confirm that column and beam locations lined up consistently between levels.

Project Overview

This academic construction-engineering assignment placed me in the role of Project Manager for a 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building on a 30 ft × 40 ft site in Charlotte, NC. I reviewed the provided architectural design, site plan, and floor plans, and independently developed a Primavera P6 construction schedule reflecting a 66-day planned construction sequence.

Design Considerations

The construction schedule required logical sequencing from substructure work through Level 1, Level 2, and roof completion, coordinated against the site plan and floor plans provided for the project. With excavation programmed for 17 days and brickwork for 30 days, early substructure activities represented a substantial portion of the 66-day schedule and directly influenced when subsequent building work could begin.

Design Approach

I organized the project into three primary construction phases, Substructure, Level 1, and Level 2, and sequenced Primavera P6 activities to reflect the building's actual layout as shown in the site and floor plans. This approach connected the provided design information to a logical construction sequence, demonstrating how project documentation supports work breakdown, activity planning, and construction scheduling.

Project Summary

This project demonstrates my ability to work across design documentation and project-controls environments by connecting a provided building design with a 66-day construction schedule. It strengthened my understanding of the relationship between site conditions, floor layout, construction sequencing, predecessor-dependent activities, and overall project duration.
