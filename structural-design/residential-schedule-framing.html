Residential Building Structural Modeling & Construction Schedule

Academic Project | Construction Engineering / BIM & Project Controls

Two-Story Residential Building

A 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building developed on a 30 ft × 40 ft lot in Charlotte, NC. I independently developed the Revit building/structural model and translated the project into a coordinated Primavera P6 construction schedule.

The structural framing layout establishes column locations and four primary elevation datums, from the footing at -10 ft through the roof at +20 ft. This reference framework kept every column and floor level consistent before the enclosed structure was modeled.

The completed structural model coordinates foundations, columns, beams, floors, and the roof into one enclosed system. Building out the full model from the framing layout made it possible to see how each level's structural components actually relate to the ones above and below it.

The Primavera P6 schedule established a 66-day construction sequence consisting of a 50-day substructure phase, 14-day Level 1 phase, and 2-day Level 2 phase. Activities shown in the schedule carry zero total float, creating a tightly constrained sequence in which delays to predecessor activities could directly affect the planned completion date.

Project Overview

This academic construction-engineering assignment placed me in the role of Project Manager for a 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building on a 30 ft × 40 ft site in Charlotte, NC. I independently developed the Revit building/structural model and Primavera P6 construction schedule, translating the provided architectural concept into coordinated building information and a 66-day planned construction sequence.

Design Considerations

The building model required coordination across multiple vertical levels, while the construction schedule required logical sequencing from substructure work through Level 1, Level 2, and roof completion. With excavation programmed for 17 days and brickwork for 30 days, early substructure activities represented a substantial portion of the 66-day schedule and directly influenced when subsequent building work could begin.

Design Approach

I organized the project into three primary construction phases, Substructure, Level 1, and Level 2, and aligned the physical building components represented in Revit with corresponding Primavera P6 activities. This approach connected the building model to a logical construction sequence, demonstrating how BIM information can support work breakdown, activity planning, and construction scheduling.
