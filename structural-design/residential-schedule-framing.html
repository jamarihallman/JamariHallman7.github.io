Residential Building Structural Modeling & Construction Schedule

Academic Project | Construction Engineering / BIM & Project Controls

From Blueprint to Build Plan

A 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building developed on a 30 ft × 40 ft lot in Charlotte, NC, as provided in the assignment brief. Green Construction Company's architects finalized this design, and my role as project manager was to review it and translate it into a coordinated Primavera P6 construction schedule using the accompanying site and floor plans.

Construction Schedule

The Primavera P6 schedule established a 66-day construction sequence broken into a 50-day substructure phase, a 14-day Level 1 phase, and a 2-day Level 2 phase. Excavation and brickwork alone accounted for 47 of the 50 substructure days, making them the activities that governed how quickly framing above could begin. Every activity in the schedule carries zero total float, meaning there was no slack anywhere in the sequence and any delay to an early activity would push the entire 66-day completion date back.

Site Plan

The site plan establishes the building's placement on the 30 ft × 40 ft lot, including setbacks, vehicle and pedestrian access, and the fenced site boundary shown in the assignment drawings. Reviewing this plan alongside the schedule made clear how the site's fixed dimensions constrained staging and access during the substructure phase, when excavation and brickwork activities needed room to proceed without interfering with the rest of the lot.

Second Floor Plan

The second floor plan lays out the upper level's room arrangement and the structural bearing points that carry down to the framing below. Coordinating this layout with the ground floor plan was necessary to confirm that column and beam locations lined up consistently between levels, since misaligned bearing points at Level 2 would have created load path problems for the Level 1 framing scheduled ahead of it.

Project Overview

This academic construction-engineering assignment placed me in the role of Project Manager for a 26 ft × 30 ft, two-story residential building on a 30 ft × 40 ft site in Charlotte, NC. I reviewed the provided architectural design, site plan, and floor plans, and independently developed a Primavera P6 construction schedule reflecting a 66-day planned construction sequence.

Design Considerations

The construction schedule required logical sequencing from substructure work through Level 1, Level 2, and roof completion, coordinated against the site plan and floor plans provided for the project. With excavation programmed for 17 days and brickwork for 30 days, early substructure activities represented a substantial portion of the 66-day schedule and directly influenced when subsequent building work could begin.

Design Approach

I organized the project into three primary construction phases, Substructure, Level 1, and Level 2, and sequenced Primavera P6 activities to reflect the building's actual layout as shown in the site and floor plans. This approach connected the provided design information to a logical construction sequence, demonstrating how project documentation supports work breakdown, activity planning, and construction scheduling.

Project Summary

This project demonstrates my ability to work across design documentation and project-controls environments by connecting a provided building design with a 66-day construction schedule. It strengthened my understanding of the relationship between site conditions, floor layout, construction sequencing, predecessor-dependent activities, and overall project duration.
