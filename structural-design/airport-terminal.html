Regional Airport Terminal Design

Structural Design | Semester-Long Academic Project | 1st Place Competition Winner

Seven-Gate Regional Airport Terminal

A seven-gate regional airport terminal developed as part of a semester long multidisciplinary airport design project that earned 1st place in competition. As Terminal Building Design & Aircraft Operations Lead, I developed the terminal's functional configuration and space program while contributing to its overall structural form and roof framing concept, coordinating the building around passenger processing requirements and gate operations.

The terminal program integrates approximately 45,000 sq. ft. of programmed passenger and operational space, including ticketing/check-in, security screening, baggage handling, concessions, restrooms, passenger circulation, support spaces, and aircraft-specific holdrooms. Individual areas were quantitatively programmed, including approximately 11,800 sq. ft. of concessions, 4,650 sq. ft. of security screening, 3,200 sq. ft. of baggage-claim support, 1,300 sq. ft. of baggage claim, and 1,100 sq. ft. of check-in, allowing the building form to develop around actual airport functions rather than an arbitrary architectural footprint.

The terminal's structural concept had to accommodate a long, changing building geometry while preserving the large, open passenger-processing and concourse spaces required for airport operations. A documented 55-ft maximum vertical envelope, established between the 960-ft site level and 1,015-ft maximum elevation, added another constraint to the development of the building and roof form, requiring architectural function, vertical clearance, and structural configuration to be considered together.

Overview

This semester-long academic project challenged our team to develop a fully functional regional airport as an integrated infrastructure system, ultimately earning 1st place in competition. My responsibility centered on terminal building design and aircraft operations, where I coordinated space programming, seven-gate terminal planning, passenger circulation, aircraft operational requirements, and the terminal's structural form and roof-framing concept rather than treating each discipline as an isolated design problem.

Design Significance

What distinguishes the terminal is the way its form responds to multiple engineering and operational systems simultaneously. Passenger processing, security, baggage movement, concessions, circulation, gate operations, building services, and structural configuration all compete for space, requiring the terminal geometry to function as both an occupied building and an operational component of the larger airport.
